[USER]
why does vk have to be mean?

[ASSISTANT]
Because V_k is trying to estimate a statistical property -- the true,
per-step innovation covariance -- from a handful of real samples, and
covariance is defined as an average squared deviation, not a sum.
Using the sum instead of the mean would break the comparison it's
built for.

Covariance is an expected value by definition: E[e.e^T]. Since only
LAMBDA_WINDOW=10 real samples are available, the standard way to
estimate an expected value from a finite sample is to average them
(the maximum-likelihood estimator for the covariance of a zero-mean
Gaussian).

Why not just sum them? A sum scales with how many samples you happen
to include -- summing 10 samples of e.e^T would be roughly 10x too
large compared to a single typical innovation, and doubling
LAMBDA_WINDOW would double the sum again with nothing in the actual
data changing.

This matters specifically because of what V_k gets compared against:
M_k = H.(F.P_prior.F^T).H^T is a single-step theoretical quantity. For
ratio = trace_N/trace_M to mean anything, V_k has to be on that same
single-step footing. The mean gives you that; the sum doesn't.
Averaging is what keeps lambda_k's scale independent of window size.

[USER]
explain then what is vk-r

[ASSISTANT]
N_k = V_k - R strips out the one piece of the real, observed spread
that's known and external -- GPS's own measurement noise -- leaving
behind only the portion of the disagreement that's actually about the
state/model.

V_k is the empirical estimate of the FULL innovation spread. In a
well-calibrated filter, that full spread theoretically breaks into
three pieces:

  S = M_k  +  H.Q.H^T  +  R
      ^         ^          ^
   carried-  this-step's  GPS's own
   over      accel        measurement
   state     noise        noise
   uncertainty

V_k (built from real residuals) reflects all three. But M_k only
captures the first piece, by design -- it excludes both Q and R.

So the subtraction is about making the comparison fair. If you skipped
-R and compared raw V_k to M_k, you'd be comparing "everything,
including GPS's fixed noise floor" against "only the state-carryover
piece" -- and since R (25-100 for this GPS setup) is typically much
bigger than M_k's steady-state range (single digits to low tens),
that comparison would come out structurally inflated almost every
step, attack or not.

Subtracting R first answers a cleaner question: "of everything
actually happening in the residuals right now, how much can't be
explained by the GPS receiver's own ordinary noise?" That's the piece
diagnostic of a model/state problem -- what needs comparing against
M_k.

my idea was till the covariance is stable the ekf.p value will keep on

does it not show up. I AM FRUSTRATED

[ASSISTANT]
[Reassured the user this was a sound, productive question, not a sign
of missing something obvious.]

Your model: disagreement persists -> P keeps inflating -> should be an
easy, growing signal. That's sound IF Kalman filtering let uncertainty
accumulate. It doesn't:

Every single step, the update forcibly resets P, regardless of how big
lambda_k just made it. This is the "collapse" mechanism: 1/P_post =
1/P_pred + 1/R (precision adds). No matter how much lambda_k inflates
P_pred this step, P_post is bounded -- it approaches R's scale from
below and can never exceed it. This happens fresh, every step, from
scratch -- there's no accumulator carrying forward "how inflated has
this been recently." Each step is: inflate briefly (pre-update) -> get
pulled back down toward R (post-update) -> repeat.

So what should actually happen during a sustained attack isn't "P
keeps growing" -- it's "P settles into a new, moderately elevated but
bounded steady state." If disagreement keeps recurring every step,
lambda_k keeps getting recomputed above 1 every step too, so P_prior
stays a bit higher than the pre-attack baseline, consistently, for as
long as the attack lasts -- but it's a shifted plateau, not a ramp.

This is actually consistent with what was seen in the
seed1_applied_stf hpmcounter3 plot earlier -- drift sitting measurably
below normal/jump in a couple of windows, not a dramatic runaway
spike. A modest, bounded shift is exactly what the real math predicts.

Suggested a concrete follow-up check: pull p_trace from the applied
variant on real hardware (DIAG-format capture, not just the
point-to-point HPC format currently in seed1_applied_stf) and check
whether it settles into a sustained but flat elevated plateau during
the attack window, versus climbing.

[USER]
so how does the update happen can you explain?

[ASSISTANT]
Walked through ekf_update() from tinyekf.h step by step, tied to this
project's concrete dimensions (EKF_N=6, EKF_M=3):

1. Innovation: z_hx = z - hx (GPS reading minus accel-driven predicted
   position).
2. Innovation covariance: S = H.P.H^T + R (total expected spread,
   same S used in compute_nis()).
3. Kalman gain: G = P.H^T.S^-1 -- computed via the Cholesky-based
   invert(), same division-heavy hot path discussed all session.
4. State update: x = x + G.(z - hx).
5. Covariance update: P = (I - G.H).P -- the "collapse" step,
   shrinking P proportional to how much the measurement actually
   contributed.

Summarized: measure the disagreement, weigh it by relative confidence,
apply the weighted correction, then shrink uncertainty to reflect what
was just learned.

[USER]
so when lambda is greater than 1, how does it bring P uncertainity
down

[ASSISTANT]
Derived the scalar Kalman precision-addition identity:
  1/p_post = 1/p + 1/r
  p_post = p.r / (p + r)

Showed that as p -> infinity (what lambda_k>1 does), p_post -> r from
below, and can never exceed r. So no matter how large lambda_k
inflates the pre-update variance, the post-update variance is capped
by the measurement noise r.

Explained why: inflating p doesn't ask the filter to "stay more
uncertain forever" -- it asks it to trust the incoming measurement
almost completely (g -> 1). Trusting a real measurement almost
entirely leaves you with roughly that measurement's own noise level,
not growing residual doubt.

Tied back to the actual code: R_mat here is SIGMA_H^2=25 / SIGMA_V^2
=100, so lambda_k inflating P pulls the posterior toward that ~25-100
range regardless of how extreme the pre-update spike was -- explaining
why the "300x spike" from the file's docstring is a momentary,
pre-update number, not what survives into p_trace.

[USER]
how are there 300 lambda k values if its windowed 10?

[ASSISTANT]
Clarified: it's a SLIDING (rolling) window, not a batch/non-overlapping
one. The window holds the most recent (up to) 10 innovations and
updates by one at a time -- drop oldest, add newest, recompute. Every
single step gets its own freshly computed lambda_k, using whichever 10
innovations are most recent as of that step. Window size controls how
much smoothing goes into each value, not how many values you get in
total.

Drew the analogy to a 10-day stock moving average: one new point
plotted every day, not one point every 10 days. Same mechanic as ANIS
(window_push()/window_mean(), WINDOW_SIZE=10) already in the firmware.

[USER]
you said lambda_k = 1 means filter has less uncertainity then why does
lambda_k go till 4 for normal operation itself

[ASSISTANT]
Clarified the paraphrase: lambda_k=1 means "no extra inflation" (a
relative baseline), not an absolute guarantee of low uncertainty. Two
built-in reasons normal legitimately produces lambda_k up to 4 (or
higher, per real hardware data):

Reason 1 -- structural bias: M_k deliberately excludes Q, but V_k
naturally includes it. So even under perfect conditions, expected
ratio = 1 + trace(H.Q.H^T)/trace(M_k) -- nudged above 1 by
construction. Confirmed by data: normal's own mean lambda_k is 1.2015,
not 1.0.

Reason 2 -- sampling noise: V_k is estimated from only 10 real
samples, so it has genuine statistical variance window to window --
sometimes a noisier-than-typical stretch of ordinary GPS noise lands
in the window, spiking ratio purely by chance.

Together: normal sits on an already-slightly-elevated baseline, with
real random jitter on top -- occasional excursions to 4 (or into the
20s on real hardware) are expected from a perfectly healthy filter,
not a sign of a problem. This is also why raw per-step lambda_k
struggles to separate drift from normal -- drift's real signal has to
clear an already-noisy baseline.
